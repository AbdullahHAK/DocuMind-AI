## Your right to appeal a denied claim

If your plan denies a claim or refuses to pay for a service (including a prior authorization request), the ACA gives you the right to appeal. There are two levels: an internal appeal, where you ask your insurance company to conduct a full and fair review of its own decision, and an external review, where an independent third party decides. The insurer must tell you in writing why it denied the claim and how to appeal.

## Internal appeal deadlines

You must file your internal appeal within 180 days (6 months) of receiving notice that your claim was denied. The insurer must decide within 30 days if the appeal is for a service you haven't received yet, and within 60 days if it is for a service you've already received. Urgent cases must be decided faster; federal claims rules generally require a decision within 72 hours for urgent care.

## External review

If the internal appeal is denied, you can request an external review by an independent reviewer. You must file a written request within 4 months of receiving the final denial. Standard external reviews are decided no later than 45 days after the request; expedited reviews for urgent situations are decided no later than 72 hours. Your insurer is required by law to accept the external reviewer's decision. Under the HHS-administered process there is no charge; other processes can charge no more than $25.

## Tips for a strong appeal

Ask the insurer for the specific plan provision used to deny the claim and a copy of your claim file. Get a letter from your doctor explaining why the treatment is medically necessary. Keep copies of everything and write down the date, name and reference number of every phone call. Check whether the denial was simply a coding or paperwork error, which can often be fixed by the provider resubmitting the claim.
