## Emergency care is an essential health benefit

Emergency services are one of the ten essential health benefits that ACA-compliant individual and small-group plans must cover.

## No prior authorization for emergencies

Plans covering emergency services can't require you to get prior approval (prior authorization) before getting emergency care, whether the hospital is in-network or out-of-network.

## Out-of-network emergency rooms

A plan can't charge you a higher copay or coinsurance for out-of-network emergency care than it would for in-network emergency care. Under the No Surprises Act, the out-of-network provider also can't bill you for the difference between their charge and what your plan pays (balance billing) for emergency services.

## The prudent layperson standard

Whether care counts as an "emergency" is judged by your symptoms, not the final diagnosis. If a reasonable person with average knowledge of health would believe the symptoms (for example severe chest pain) needed immediate care to avoid serious harm, the visit should be treated as an emergency, even if it turns out to be indigestion.
