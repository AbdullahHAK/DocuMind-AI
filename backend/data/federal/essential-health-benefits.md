## The ten essential health benefits

ACA-compliant individual and small-group plans must cover ten categories of essential health benefits: outpatient (ambulatory) care; emergency services; hospitalization; pregnancy, maternity and newborn care; mental health and substance use disorder services, including behavioral health treatment; prescription drugs; rehabilitative and habilitative services and devices; laboratory services; preventive and wellness services and chronic disease management; and pediatric services, including oral and vision care for children.

## What is usually not required

Adult dental care and adult vision care (such as routine eye exams and glasses for adults) are not essential health benefits, so many medical plans exclude them; they are often sold as separate plans. Cosmetic procedures, long-term custodial care, and services that aren't medically necessary are also commonly excluded.

## No annual or lifetime dollar limits

Plans can't put a yearly or lifetime dollar cap on coverage of essential health benefits. This doesn't stop a plan from limiting the number of visits for some services (for example 20 physical therapy visits per year), as long as the plan follows other federal rules such as mental health parity.

## Large employer and self-funded plans

Large-group and self-funded employer plans are not required to cover every essential health benefit category, but if they do cover one, they can't put annual or lifetime dollar limits on it.
