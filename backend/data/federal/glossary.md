title: Health Insurance Glossary

## Deductible

The amount you pay for covered health care services before your insurance plan starts to pay. With a $3,000 deductible, for example, you pay the first $3,000 of covered services yourself. Some services, such as preventive care or copay-based office visits, may be covered before you meet the deductible.

## Copayment (copay)

A fixed amount (for example $30) you pay for a covered service, usually when you get the service. Whether copays apply before or after the deductible depends on the plan.

## Coinsurance

Your share of the cost of a covered service, calculated as a percentage of the allowed amount. If the allowed amount for a visit is $1,000 and your coinsurance is 30%, you pay $300 once your deductible is met; the plan pays the rest.

## Allowed amount

The maximum amount a plan will pay for a covered service, sometimes called the negotiated rate for in-network providers. An out-of-network provider may bill you for the difference between their charge and the allowed amount (balance billing), unless the No Surprises Act protects you.

## In-network and out-of-network

In-network providers have a contract with your plan to accept negotiated rates, so you pay less. Out-of-network providers have no contract; you usually pay more, and some plans (HMOs, EPOs) don't cover out-of-network care at all except emergencies.

## Medically necessary

Health care services needed to diagnose or treat an illness, injury, condition or its symptoms, and that meet accepted standards of medicine. Plans can deny claims they consider not medically necessary; that decision can be appealed.

## Formulary

The list of prescription drugs covered by a plan, usually organised into tiers (for example generic, preferred brand, non-preferred brand, specialty) with different costs.

## Step therapy

A rule requiring you to try a lower-cost drug first and have it fail before the plan covers a more expensive drug for the same condition. You or your doctor can request an exception.
