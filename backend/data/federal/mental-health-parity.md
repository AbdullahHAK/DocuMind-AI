## What parity means

The Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act (MHPAEA) requires that coverage for mental health and substance use disorder (MH/SUD) benefits be no more restrictive than coverage for medical and surgical benefits. It applies to most employer group plans and to individual market plans.

## What it covers in practice

Copays, coinsurance, deductibles and out-of-pocket limits for MH/SUD care can't be higher than those for comparable medical care. Visit limits, prior authorization requirements and other "treatment limitations" for MH/SUD care can't be stricter than those applied to comparable medical/surgical care. Example: if a plan doesn't limit the number of visits to a primary care doctor, it generally can't cap therapy visits at 20 per year.

## How to use this right

If you think your plan treats mental health care more strictly than medical care, you can ask the plan in writing for the criteria it uses for medical necessity and for comparable medical/surgical benefits; plans must provide this information. Employer plan members can contact the U.S. Department of Labor's Employee Benefits Security Administration (EBSA) for help.
