## What the No Surprises Act protects you from

Since January 1, 2022, the No Surprises Act protects people with most private health plans from surprise out-of-network bills ("balance billing") for: emergency services, including at out-of-network emergency rooms; non-emergency care from out-of-network providers at an in-network hospital, hospital outpatient department, or ambulatory surgical center; and air ambulance services from out-of-network providers.

## You pay only in-network cost-sharing

In those protected situations, you can only be charged your plan's in-network cost-sharing (copay, coinsurance, deductible). Those payments count toward your in-network deductible and out-of-pocket maximum.

## Providers who can't ask you to waive your protections

Some out-of-network providers at in-network facilities may ask you to sign a notice giving up these protections for non-emergency care. But certain providers can never ask you to waive them: emergency medicine, anesthesiology, pathology, radiology, neonatology, assistant surgeons, hospitalists, intensivists, and diagnostic lab services. You are never required to give up your protections to get care.

## What is not covered by the law

Ground ambulance services are not covered by the No Surprises Act's balance billing protections (some states have their own ground ambulance laws). The law also doesn't apply to services you knowingly chose to receive out-of-network outside of the protected situations.

## Good faith estimates and getting help

If you are uninsured or not using insurance, providers must give you a good faith estimate of costs. If your final bill is at least $400 more than the estimate, you may be able to dispute it. For questions or complaints about surprise bills, call the No Surprises Help Desk at 1-800-985-3059.
