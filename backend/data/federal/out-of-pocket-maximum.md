## What the out-of-pocket maximum is

The out-of-pocket maximum (also called out-of-pocket limit) is the most you have to pay for covered, in-network services in a plan year. After you spend this amount on deductibles, copayments, and coinsurance, your plan pays 100% of the costs of covered essential health benefits for the rest of the year.

## Federal limit for 2026

For 2026 plan years, the out-of-pocket maximum for a Marketplace plan can be no more than $10,600 for an individual and $21,200 for a family. The limit is updated every year, and most non-grandfathered employer plans are held to the same limits.

## What counts and what doesn't

Your deductible, copayments and coinsurance for covered in-network services count toward the out-of-pocket maximum. Your monthly premiums don't count. Spending on out-of-network care (outside No Surprises Act situations) and on services the plan doesn't cover usually doesn't count either.

## Family plans have an individual cap

In a family plan, no single person can be required to pay more than the individual out-of-pocket limit, even if the family limit hasn't been reached yet. This is sometimes called an "embedded" individual out-of-pocket maximum.
