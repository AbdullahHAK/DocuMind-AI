## HMO, PPO, EPO and POS plans

An HMO usually limits coverage to in-network providers (except emergencies) and often requires a referral from your primary care doctor to see a specialist. A PPO pays more for in-network care but still covers some out-of-network care, usually without referrals. An EPO covers in-network care only (except emergencies) but usually doesn't require referrals. A POS plan pays more for in-network care and requires referrals for specialists.

## The Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC)

Every plan must give you a Summary of Benefits and Coverage: a standard, easy-to-compare summary (no more than 8 pages, in a government-required format) of costs and coverage, including coverage examples. You can request it from your insurer or employer at any time, free of charge. The SBC is a summary; the full plan document (certificate, evidence of coverage, or plan document) has the complete rules and controls if the two disagree.

## Self-funded employer plans and ERISA

Many large employers "self-fund" their health plans: the employer pays claims directly and an insurance company only administers the plan. Self-funded private-employer plans are governed by the federal ERISA law and the U.S. Department of Labor, so state insurance mandates and state insurance departments generally don't apply to them. Fully insured plans are regulated by your state's insurance department. Your plan document or HR department can tell you which kind you have, which matters when deciding where to file a complaint.
