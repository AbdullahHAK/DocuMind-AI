## Preventive care must be covered at no cost

Under the Affordable Care Act (ACA), most health plans must cover a set of preventive services with no cost-sharing: no copay, no coinsurance, and no deductible, even if you haven't met your deductible yet. This applies when the service is delivered by an in-network provider. Grandfathered plans (plans that existed before March 23, 2010 and haven't changed significantly) are exempt.

## Which services count as preventive

The covered list comes from federal expert bodies: screenings and counseling rated "A" or "B" by the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force (for example blood pressure, cholesterol, colorectal cancer, depression and diabetes screenings), vaccines recommended by the CDC's advisory committee (ACIP), and preventive care for women and children recommended by HRSA (for example well-woman visits, FDA-approved contraception, breastfeeding support, well-baby and well-child visits).

## When you may still be charged

You can be charged cost-sharing if the visit's main purpose wasn't preventive, if the provider is out-of-network, or if the screening finds something and you get follow-up diagnostic tests or treatment. Example: an annual physical is free, but if you also ask the doctor to examine a new knee injury during that visit, the knee part can be billed as a regular office visit. Ask your provider to code preventive services correctly if you receive a bill.

## Colonoscopy after a positive stool test

Federal guidance says that a follow-up colonoscopy after a positive non-invasive colorectal cancer screening test (such as a stool-based test) is part of the preventive screening and must be covered without cost-sharing.
