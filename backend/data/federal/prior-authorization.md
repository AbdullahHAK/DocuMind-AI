## What prior authorization is

Prior authorization (also called preauthorization, precertification or prior approval) is a decision by your health plan that a service, treatment plan, prescription drug or medical equipment is medically necessary before you receive it. Plans often require it for advanced imaging (MRI, CT, PET), planned hospital stays and surgeries, specialty drugs, and some therapies. Approval isn't a promise the plan will pay; the claim must still meet the plan's other terms.

## What happens if you skip it

If your plan requires prior authorization and nobody gets it, the plan can reduce or deny payment, even for a service it would otherwise cover. In-network providers usually request authorization for you, but it's worth confirming before the appointment. A prior authorization denial can be appealed like any other claim denial.

## Faster decision deadlines for some plans (2026)

A 2024 federal rule (CMS Interoperability and Prior Authorization Final Rule) requires certain payers, including Medicare Advantage plans, Medicaid and CHIP programs, and insurers selling Marketplace plans on HealthCare.gov, to decide prior authorization requests within 72 hours for urgent requests and 7 calendar days for standard requests, starting in 2026, and to give a specific reason for denials. Employer plans are not covered by this rule.

## Emergencies never need prior authorization

Plans that cover emergency services can't require prior authorization for emergency care, in-network or out-of-network.
